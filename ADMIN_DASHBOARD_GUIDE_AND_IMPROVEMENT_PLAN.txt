KAM FLEETS
ADMIN DASHBOARD: PLAIN-LANGUAGE GUIDE AND IMPROVEMENT PLAN

Purpose
-------
This document explains what the Admin Dashboard shows, where its numbers come
from, what a manager can do there, how that differs from day-to-day operations,
and a practical plan for making the screen easier to understand.

The explanations below describe the dashboard's reporting behavior.

Implemented improvements include:
  - The key money cards and report period stay visible while Overview, Billing,
    Trip costs, and Review are switched.
  - The dashboard opens to an automatically calculated Today summary and moves
    its date boundary forward at local midnight.
  - The first screen shows the three main chart measures; budget estimates are
    optional, and monthly figures have a text/table alternative.
  - Review items are prioritized, summarized at the top, and expandable to the
    full list. Cost breakdowns, payment transactions, and service records can
    be opened when needed.
  - Invoices require an entered amount or an explicit choice to use the trip
    budget estimate. Payment entry shows and validates the remaining balance.
  - Completed-trip budgets and saved costs are included by completion date,
    rather than by a later invoice or payment date.
  - Trip screens explain that budget variance is not customer revenue or profit.

Production-readiness work such as authenticated shared records and protected
server-side audit history remains a recommendation.


1. THE SIMPLEST WAY TO THINK ABOUT THE TWO DASHBOARDS
----------------------------------------------------
Think of the Operations Dashboard as the driver's windscreen: it helps people
see what is happening now and what is about to happen.

Think of the Admin Dashboard as the manager's office: it looks back across
trips and money records so a manager can review budgets, costs, bills, payments,
and missing information.

The Operations Dashboard focuses on:
  - Trips that are currently ongoing.
  - Planned trips whose departure time is still in the future.
  - Trucks being used, delays on ongoing trips, and trip expenses/parts.
  - Open trip budget estimates and quick access to plan or update trips.

The Admin Dashboard focuses on:
  - Completed-trip budget estimates and finalized trip costs.
  - Customer invoices, money collected, and unpaid invoice balances.
  - Fleet-service costs, which are kept separate from trip costs.
  - Date and trip-status filters, charts, detailed trip records, and CSV export.
  - Cost-reconciliation checks, data-quality warnings, and a change history.

The app currently has separate screens, not separate secured job roles. There
is no login/permission system that prevents an Operations user from opening
Admin or entering invoices. "Admin" describes a dashboard and its tools; it is
not currently a security boundary.


2. HOW TO READ THE MONEY NUMBERS
--------------------------------
All currency amounts are displayed in Tanzanian shillings (Tsh).
Different money labels mean different things. They must not be added together
as though they all mean money earned.

TRIP BUDGET ESTIMATE
  The planned amount of money set aside for a trip.
  It is a plan, not a customer bill and not proof that money was received.

INVOICED
  The amount written on invoices issued to customers.
  This is the amount billed. It is not necessarily paid yet.

CASH COLLECTED
  The total of payment records received during the selected report period.
  A partial payment counts only for the amount recorded, not for the whole bill.

OUTSTANDING BALANCE
  The amount still unpaid on invoices issued during the selected period.
  The dashboard uses all recorded payments to calculate the current balance,
  even if some of those payments happened after the selected report period.

RECORDED COSTS
  Recorded trip expenses, in-trip parts, driver pay for completed trips, and
  separate fleet-service transactions, subject to the report rules below.
  This is the cost information recorded in the app, not a guarantee that every
  real-world cost has been entered.

FINALIZED TRIP BUDGET VARIANCE
  For a completed trip:
    trip budget estimate - finalized expenses - finalized parts - driver pay
  A positive number means the recorded trip costs were less than the budget
  estimate. A negative number means they were higher than that estimate.
  This is a comparison against a budget. It is NOT accounting profit, billed
  revenue, or cash in the bank.

WITHIN-BUDGET TRIPS
  Counts completed trips whose finalized budget variance is zero or positive.
  It does not mean the customer paid the bill or that the company made a profit.


3. WHERE THE NUMBERS COME FROM
------------------------------
TRIP BUDGET
  Comes from each trip's budget_allocated value.
  The completed-trip budget card adds the budgets of completed trips included
  by the current report filters.

TRIP EXPENSES
  Examples include fuel, tolls, and other trip expenses entered against a trip.

IN-TRIP PARTS
  Spare parts and related repair costs recorded against a trip.

DRIVER PAY
  The trip's driver-pay amount is included in recorded trip costs only after
  the trip is completed. A planned trip may show an estimate in its detail, but
  that estimate is not counted as a cost yet.

FINALIZED COST SNAPSHOTS
  When a trip is completed, the app saves finalized expense and parts totals.
  The completed-trip cost totals use those saved totals. The expandable ledger
  detail also lists linked expense and parts records and shows a snapshot
  adjustment if their sum does not match the saved final total.

FLEET-SERVICE COSTS
  Maintenance/service records attached to trucks are added as their own cost
  category. They are not assigned to a trip, so they are not included in an
  individual trip's budget variance.

INVOICES AND PAYMENTS
  An invoice is linked to a completed trip. It records an invoice number,
  billed amount, issue date, and due date. One invoice may have several partial
  payments. Each payment records an amount, method, received date, and optional
  reference. The app prevents a payment from exceeding the remaining invoice
  balance through its normal application workflow.


4. HOW REPORT FILTERS AND DATES WORK
------------------------------------
The Admin report can be filtered by:
  - All time, last 30 days, last 90 days, year to date, or a custom date range.
  - All statuses, completed, ongoing, or planned.
  - Text search in the trip ledger for route, driver, truck plate, or cargo.

Different records use different dates:
  - Completed trip budgets and finalized costs normally use the trip completion
    date, with an important exception explained below.
  - Open-trip expenses and parts use their transaction dates.
  - Invoices use their issue dates.
  - Payments use the dates money was received.
  - Fleet-service costs use their service transaction dates.

Important current behavior to know:
  The report first decides which trips to include. A trip can be included when
  its trip date, an open-trip cost date, an invoice issue date, or a linked
  payment date is inside the selected range. Once a completed trip is included,
  its budget and full saved finalized trip-cost snapshot are counted. Therefore,
  if an old completed trip appears in a period because it had a new invoice or
  payment, its old budget and finalized trip costs can also appear in that
  period's summary cards. This can make those cards look like pure
  "activity during this period" totals when they are not.

The collected-cash figure is based on payments received in the selected period
for invoices belonging to trips that match the status filter. It can include
a payment for an invoice issued before the selected period. This is sensible
for cash received in a period, but should be explained by the interface.

The monthly chart always shows only six months ending at the report's end date
(or today for an all-time report); it is not an all-time chart. Its completed
trip budget and finalized-cost bars are placed in the completion month. Open
trip costs and fleet servicing use transaction month, invoices use issue month,
and collections use received month.


5. WHAT THE ADMIN USER CAN DO
-----------------------------
The Admin Dashboard currently lets a user:
  1. Choose a time range and trip status for the report.
  2. Review summary cards, a six-month chart, and cost-category totals.
  3. Review invoices with amount, amount received, remaining balance, status,
     and due date.
  4. Issue an invoice for a completed trip. If the amount is left blank, the
     current form uses that trip's budget estimate as the invoice amount.
     A manager should check the amount carefully; a budget is not automatically
     the correct customer price.
  5. Record full or partial payments against an unpaid invoice, including
     payment method, received date, and optional reference.
  6. Expand a trip in the ledger to inspect its costs, snapshot adjustments,
     budget comparison, invoices, payments, and outstanding amount.
  7. Open the full trip record from the ledger.
  8. Export filtered trip-ledger information as a CSV spreadsheet.
  9. Review cost-reconciliation and data-quality messages, such as missing
     assignments, missing invoices/final calculations, overdue invoices,
     overpayments, or differences between final snapshots and line items.
 10. Review audit-history events and set the operator name used to attribute
     new events.

Audit-history limitation:
  The operator name is typed into the browser and is not verified. Records are
  stored locally in that browser, so this is useful as a demo/change history,
  but it is not a tamper-proof legal audit record.


6. HOW THE DAILY USER IS DIFFERENT
----------------------------------
A day-to-day operations user usually needs to answer questions such as:
  - Which trips are on the road?
  - Which trips leave soon?
  - Is a truck or driver assigned?
  - Is there a delay or a roadside problem?
  - What trip expense or replacement part must be recorded?
  - What trip needs to be created or updated?

The Operations Dashboard is designed around those current tasks. Other
navigation screens let users work with trips, trucks, drivers, expenses, parts,
and truck services.

An Admin user is more likely to ask:
  - How much did we bill in this period?
  - How much cash did we actually record receiving?
  - Which bills still have balances?
  - What did completed trips cost compared with their estimates?
  - Which records need checking?
  - Can I review or export the details behind the summary?

These are workflow differences, not enforced access differences yet. The same
app data and navigation are available in the browser. If the business needs
separate daily-user and manager permissions, authenticated accounts and
server-enforced roles must be added.


7. HOW TO MAKE THE ADMIN DASHBOARD EASIER TO READ
-------------------------------------------------
The dashboard currently puts filters, eight summary cards, a four-series
chart, cost categories, invoice/payment tables and forms, reconciliation,
quality warnings, a detailed trip ledger, and audit history on one long page.
That provides transparency, but it can feel crowded.

Recommended simpler layout:

A. KEEP THE FIRST SCREEN FOR THE MOST IMPORTANT ANSWERS
  Show four main money cards first:
    - Cash collected
    - Outstanding balance
    - Invoiced
    - Recorded costs
  Put the report period beside or immediately above these cards so the user
  can always tell what dates the numbers describe.

B. KEEP ESTIMATES CLEARLY SEPARATE
  Place completed-trip budget and budget variance in a separate "Trip budget"
  area, not mixed into a row that could be mistaken for actual revenue/profit.
  Keep the word "estimate" visible in the label.

C. GROUP THE REST INTO CLEAR SECTIONS
  Use simple tabs or collapsible sections:
    - Overview: key cards and a simple trend chart.
    - Billing: invoices, payments, and balances.
    - Trip costs: reconciliation and the trip ledger.
    - Review: warnings and audit history.
  Keep the key totals visible when switching sections if practical.

D. SHOW IMPORTANT WARNINGS, NOT EVERY DETAIL, AT ONCE
  At the top, show a small count such as "3 items need review." List the most
  important issues first and provide a "View all" action. Keep resolved or
  lower-priority details out of the way without deleting their records.

E. MAKE DETAILS OPTIONAL, NOT HIDDEN FROM THE USER
  Keep an expandable trip cost breakdown, invoice transactions, and service
  records. Start with a clear summary and let the user open detail only when
  they need to check how a number was built.

F. USE PLAIN WORDS AND SHORT DEFINITIONS
  Add a small help icon or "What does this mean?" text beside terms such as
  "budget variance," "outstanding," and "finalized costs." State explicitly
  that budget variance is not profit.

G. SIMPLIFY THE CHART
  Start with three clearly named measures: Invoiced, Cash collected, and
  Recorded costs. Put budget estimate behind an optional toggle or show it in
  the separate budget section. Use direct labels and a table/text alternative
  so the chart is understandable without relying on color.

H. MAKE ACCOUNTING TASKS FEEL LIKE SHORT WIZARDS
  Keep "Issue invoice" and "Record payment" as clear actions. Ask for only
  required details first, show the selected trip/invoice and remaining balance,
  and confirm the result. Do not silently treat a trip budget as the right
  invoice amount; make the manager enter or explicitly confirm the billed
  amount.


8. FRONTEND IMPROVEMENT PLAN
----------------------------
PHASE 1 - CLARITY AND TRUST
  - Keep the labels "budget estimate", "invoiced", "cash collected",
    "outstanding balance", and "recorded costs" distinct everywhere.
  - Rename any user-facing "profit/loss" language to "budget variance" when it
    describes budget minus finalized trip costs.
  - Show the active date range and status filter beside the results.
  - Give each money card a one-sentence definition and date rule.
  - Change invoice entry so a budget amount is not silently used as the price;
    require an explicit amount or an explicit confirmation of a suggested one.
  - Explain that current data and operator attribution are browser-local.

PHASE 2 - FIX REPORT PERIOD MEANING
  - Calculate each measure from records dated for that measure, instead of
    including a completed trip's full historical cost snapshot just because a
    recent invoice or payment brought the trip into the report.
  - Make the date rule visible for each card and chart series.
  - Keep cash received in a period separate from invoices issued in a period.
  - Decide and display whether outstanding means "current balance on invoices
    issued in this period" or "balance as of the end of this period."
  - Add tests for date boundaries, partial payments, and payments made after a
    report period.

PHASE 3 - REORGANIZE THE PAGE
  - Implement the Overview, Billing, Trip costs, and Review sections described
    above.
  - Keep the main summary short; move detailed tables and the audit timeline
    lower or behind a section control.
  - Sort warnings by importance and link each warning directly to the record
    that needs review.
  - Keep CSV export available from the ledger area and identify which filters
    the export uses.

PHASE 4 - MOBILE AND ACCESSIBILITY
  - On small screens, use readable cards instead of forcing users to scan a
    wide spreadsheet table. Keep a downloadable CSV for spreadsheet analysis.
  - On medium and large screens, use a table with clear column names, spacing,
    and optional sticky headers.
  - Check that text has strong contrast and is not too small.
  - Use both a text label and an icon/color for status; never use color alone.
  - Support keyboard use, visible focus, screen-reader labels, and chart text
    summaries.
  - Test narrow phones, larger phones, tablets, laptops, and desktop displays.

PHASE 5 - PRODUCTION READINESS (WHEN REQUIRED)
  - Move shared business records from browser localStorage to an authenticated
    server/database so multiple users see the same data.
  - Add verified sign-in and server-side roles if daily and Admin access should
    differ.
  - Attribute audit entries to the signed-in user on the server; make audit
    history append-only and protected from browser edits.
  - Verify database constraints and invoice/payment behavior against the
    production PostgreSQL setup before deployment.


9. RECOMMENDED FIRST RELEASE OF THE CLEANER SCREEN
-------------------------------------------------
For the first visual simplification, do these items in order:
  1. Clarify the report date rules and fix cost totals to follow cost dates.
  2. Show four top cards: cash collected, outstanding, invoiced, recorded costs.
  3. Move budget estimates and variance into a separate trip-budget section.
  4. Collapse or move detailed billing transactions, reconciliation, audit
     history, and the full ledger into their own sections.
  5. Require the user to confirm the invoice amount rather than automatically
     using a trip budget.
  6. Recheck the layout on phone, tablet, and desktop sizes.

This order keeps the most important promise of the Admin Dashboard: the reader
can tell what each number means, which dates it covers, and where to look to
verify it.
